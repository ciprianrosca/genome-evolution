# Does the resume's summary speak for its owner and fit the job?

You are reviewing the summary at the top of a tailored resume (the paragraph under its "Summary" heading), next to
the job description it was tailored for. The person and the companies are made up. Other checks have already made
sure every claim on the resume comes from the person's fact bank, so don't judge whether the facts are true.

The summary passes when both of these hold:

1. It is written in the first person, the way people write their own resume: "I lead…" or a phrase with no subject
   such as "Engineering leader who…". It fails when it talks about the person from the outside, by name or as
   "she", "they" or "the candidate".
2. It fits this job: it leads with the experience the job description asks for most, in the job's own terms where
   they are honest, and leaves out what has nothing to do with the role. A generic summary that would read the same
   for any job fails.

Length and style are not judged here, unless the summary is so long that its point is lost.
